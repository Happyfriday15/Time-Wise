# Project brief: Time Wise

## The competition

- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8).
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: December 18th, 2026, 5pm eastern. Have to ask teacher to register. December 23rd, 2026, 5pm eastern. Have to submit the project, but aim to finish early.

## The problem

- People in my school struggle with controlling their screen time because some apps could be too addicting.
- Impact area: School and learning.
- Why it matters (impact): If my project works, the people in my school will stop doomscrolling for hours or playing video games for hours. Their lives will be better since they can go outside and get fresh air, while also avoiding bad posture and nearsightedness.
- Evidence it’s real: About 1 in 4 teenagers with 4 hours or more of daily screen time have experienced anxiety (27.1%) or depression symptoms (25.9%) in the past 2 weeks. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db513.htm
- What people told me: Drew said that he has a problem with Minecraft addiction, playing all morning and some of the afternoon this Saturday, well exceeding 4 hours.

## My solution

- My AI helps by having a changing limit based on a person's day, like homework, sleep, and the person's goals. It works better than normal limits because normal limits do not know what the person has been through and are programmed to be the same every day. Another way the AI can help is by asking what the person is going to do when going into an app, creating a pause for people to think about. The last thing an AI can do is ask questions when people ignore screen limits to help them get back on track.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Apple screen limits and Android screen limits. They are not as flexible because they can be bypassed, and the limits are the same every day unless the user changes them in settings. Mine asks the user what they are going to do before using an app, so they pause and think first.
- Safety plan: The AI may give a limit that is too strict. The solution is that it is only a suggestion, the user can change it, and the AI can adjust it based on the user's answers. Another problem might be that the user asks the AI about life problems like bullying or mental health. The AI will tell the user to get help from a trusted adult or therapist because the AI might give an unhelpful response.

## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)

1. The user can view a history of how much time they spent on each app.
2. The user can set personalized screen time limits based on their daily activities.
3. The user can be reminded to take regular breaks during long periods of screen use.
4. The user can receive reminders before their screen time limit is reached.
5. The user can manually adjust suggested screen time limits if they feel it is too strict.
6. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## Maybe later (not yet)

- The user can choose to have the AI suggest fun activities outside of screen time.
- The user can receive encouragement messages for staying within their limits.
- The user can disable certain apps temporarily if they need a break from them.
- The user can provide feedback to the AI to improve its suggestions over time.

## My AI’s instructions

- When it answers: The user answers a checklist for the AI to give it a screen time and presses Done.
- Its job: To help the user make a screen time limit.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: The user; it should sound polite, and the user should decide the screen time, not the AI.
- Facts it must use: The user's answers from the survey it gives the user.
- Rules it must follow: It should never do things without the user's consent, such as giving them a screen time limit without the user saying yes. It should not say rude things to the user.
- How to answer: The answer should not be long, so the user does not get tired reading it. It should say okay and recommend other options. If the user says no, it says okay and uses that limit. If the user chooses an option, the AI says okay and uses that option.
